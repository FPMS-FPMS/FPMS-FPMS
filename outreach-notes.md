# Cultural outreach notes

**Purpose.** This document exists so that the way FPMS approaches Indigenous communities is *documented*, not just claimed. If someone reads our code and wants to know whether the "cultural heritage" framing is real or decorative, this file is the answer.

---

## The principle

FPMS is a project about protecting cultural heritage sites from wildfire, with a particular focus on Indigenous cultural burning practices as prevention. That framing carries responsibility. We are two Grade 7 students. We are not from the communities whose land, knowledge, and heritage this project centers. What we can do is approach carefully — to learn, not to extract.

Three commitments guide every step:

1. **Listen first, build second.** Every outreach conversation is exploratory. No project pitch until the person we're speaking with knows we're there to understand what matters to them, not to convince them of anything.

2. **Never solicit Indigenous communities for competition/trip funding.** This would invert the relationship and signal tokenism. Financial support for the WRO trip comes from non-Indigenous sources.

3. **Communities decide, not the robot.** FPMS *documents* heritage sites and *maps* risk. Every decision about which sites are included, what the records say, whether cultural burning is appropriate, and where — belongs to the communities involved. This is enforced in the software: there is no algorithmic "burn recommendation" output, only a risk map made available for people to interpret.

## The three-cups-of-tea approach

Our outreach follows a patient, relationship-first pattern:

- **First cup:** introduce ourselves as people, not as a project. Explain honestly why we are reaching out. Ask nothing beyond a conversation.
- **Second cup:** listen. Whatever the person we've reached wants to share about what matters to them, their community, their relationship with the land — that's the whole point.
- **Third cup:** only then, and only if the conversation invites it, share what we're working on and ask if any of it seems useful.

We are early in this process. This is the correct pace.

## What we will NOT do

- ❌ Claim community endorsement we do not have
- ❌ Include Indigenous imagery, symbols, or names in our robots, dashboard, or marketing without explicit permission
- ❌ Publish heritage site coordinates on the public dashboard by default — heritage records are private unless a community explicitly wants them public
- ❌ Frame ourselves as "helping" communities in ways they haven't asked to be helped
- ❌ Present cultural burning as a technical problem the robot solves — it is a cultural practice the robot can support with data, if invited to

## What we hope to learn

If a conversation happens, the questions we bring are simple:

- Does the framing of this project make sense from where you sit?
- Is there anything we should be doing differently, or not doing at all?
- What would useful actually look like, in the specific context you know best?

The answers to those questions matter more than any technical feature we could add.
